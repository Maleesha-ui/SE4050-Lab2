1. What happens when the number of hidden nodes increase?
As the number of hidden nodes increases, the neural network gains the ability to learn more complex and non-linear patterns in the data. This generally leads to an increase in prediction accuracy. In our experimental results, the accuracy improved significantly from 67.5% with 1 hidden unit to 91.25% with 5 hidden units, demonstrating that adding more hidden nodes allows the model to fit the training data better.

2. Can you explain the pattern of the accuracy when the hidden nodes increase?
The pattern of accuracy follows a clear trend:

With very few hidden nodes (e.g., 1 or 2): The model suffers from underfitting. It is too simple to capture the complex structure of the "flower" dataset, resulting in lower accuracy (around 67%).

As hidden nodes increase (e.g., 3 to 5): The accuracy rises sharply, reaching a peak of 91.25% at 5 hidden units. The model becomes capable of learning the decision boundary effectively.

With a very large number of hidden nodes (e.g., 20 or 50): The accuracy starts to plateau or slightly decrease (e.g., dropping to 90.25% at 50 hidden units). This indicates overfitting, where the model memorizes the training data too closely and loses some ability to generalize.

Therefore, the optimal hidden layer size for this specific dataset is around 4 to 5 nodes, balancing between underfitting and overfitting.
